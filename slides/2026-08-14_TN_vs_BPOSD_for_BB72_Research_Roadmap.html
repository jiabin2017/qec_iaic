Where Tensor Networks Can Contribute Beyond BP+OSD for BB72

A research roadmap for identifying when logical-coset tensor-network decoding is genuinely useful for BB72, rather than simply reproducing what BP+OSD already does well.
Context. The companion document, Tensor-Network Decoder for BB72, explains how to construct the logical-coset tensor network for BB72 and how to compute \(Z_\lambda(s)\). The present document addresses the next question: if BP+OSD is already a strong decoder for BB72, what exactly should tensor networks contribute?

1. The central research question

For BB72 under a simple code-capacity noise model, BP+OSD can already be a very strong practical decoder. Therefore the goal should not be

\[ \boxed{ \text{“replace BP+OSD everywhere with a global tensor-network decoder.”} } \]

A much sharper goal is

\[ \boxed{ \text{identify the syndromes and noise regimes where BP+OSD does not recover the most probable logical coset,} } \]

and then determine whether tensor-network coset summation can recover that missing information at acceptable cost.

The tensor network should be treated first as a logical-coset probability engine and an oracle for decoding optimality, not immediately as a replacement for a fast production decoder.

2. What BP+OSD and TN are actually computing

2.1 BP+OSD

Given a syndrome \(s\), belief propagation uses the Tanner graph and the assumed noise model to estimate qubit reliabilities or approximate marginal probabilities. OSD then uses these reliabilities to construct a finite set of syndrome-consistent candidate errors.

Conceptually, standard BP+OSD is close to

\[ e_{\mathrm{BP+OSD}} \approx \arg\max_{e:\,H_Ze^T=s} P(e), \]

or equivalently, under uniform independent \(X\)-noise, to selecting a highly likely / low-cost individual error representative.

It can work extremely well, but it does not generally evaluate the full probability mass of every logical coset.

2.2 Tensor-network logical-coset decoding

For a fixed syndrome \(s\), choose one representative \(e_0\) and one logical label \(\lambda\). The logical coset is

\[ e_0+\lambda L_X+\mathcal S_X. \]

The TN decoder evaluates

\[ \boxed{ Z_\lambda(s) = \sum_{g\in\mathcal S_X} P(e_0+\lambda L_X+g). } \]

The logical posterior is

\[ P(\lambda|s) = \frac{Z_\lambda(s)} {\sum_\mu Z_\mu(s)}, \]

and the optimal logical decision is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_{\rm ML} = \arg\max_\lambda Z_\lambda(s). } \]

Therefore the conceptual distinction is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{BP+OSD: search for good physical-error candidates} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{TN: sum the probability of an entire logical equivalence class.} } \]

3. Why can these two decisions differ?

Suppose two logical sectors, \(A\) and \(B\), are compatible with the same syndrome.

Sector \(A\) may contain one especially probable low-weight error:

\[ P(e_A)=0.09, \]

while the remainder of that coset contributes only a small amount:

\[ Z_A\simeq 0.10. \]

Sector \(B\) may contain no single error as probable as \(e_A\), but many moderately probable stabilizer-equivalent errors:

\[ 0.04,\quad 0.04,\quad 0.04,\quad \ldots \]

so that

\[ Z_B\simeq0.12. \]

A decoder focused on the best individual candidate can favor sector \(A\), while degenerate maximum-likelihood decoding should select sector \(B\).

3.1 Weight enumerator viewpoint

For independent \(X\)-noise, define

\[ A_{\lambda,w}(s) = \#\{ e:\, H_Ze^T=s,\, e\in\lambda,\, w(e)=w \}. \]

Then

\[ Z_\lambda(s;p) = \sum_w A_{\lambda,w}(s) p^w(1-p)^{72-w}. \]

A minimum-weight or candidate-search viewpoint emphasizes the smallest \(w\), whereas the full coset probability also depends on the multiplicity \(A_{\lambda,w}\).

This is the useful statistical-mechanics analogy:

\[ \boxed{ \text{individual-error weight} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{energy}, } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{number of equivalent errors} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{entropy}. } \]

The logical-coset decision depends on the combined free-energy-like quantity, not only on the minimum weight.

4. Where BP+OSD is likely already sufficient

Tensor networks should not be expected to provide meaningful practical gains in every regime.

Regime Expected role of BP+OSD Expected role of TN
Very low \(p\), simple isolated errors Usually strong; one low-weight pattern dominates Little additional benefit
Easy syndromes with clear BP convergence Fast and usually reliable Probably unnecessary
Routine real-time decoding of all shots Natural production decoder Global TN likely too expensive
Very high \(p\), beyond useful decoding regime Performance degrades MLD itself may not recover much information
A negative result is scientifically useful. If BB72 under iid code-capacity noise shows almost no gap between BP+OSD and logical-coset MLD, then the correct conclusion is that this regime is not the place to deploy TN.

5. The most promising region: hard syndromes

The first place to look is not the average syndrome but the subset for which BP or BP+OSD becomes uncertain or structurally unreliable.

Examples include:

  • BP non-convergence;
  • oscillatory or unstable messages;
  • short-loop / trapping-set-like structures;
  • many low-reliability qubits clustered in one region;
  • small likelihood gap between the best OSD candidates;
  • multiple logical sectors with comparable posterior mass.

For these syndromes, TN may add value because it can perform an explicit or controlled approximation to the stabilizer sum rather than relying only on local messages and a finite candidate list.

The first practical hypothesis to test is: \[ \boxed{ \text{Most BB72 syndromes are easy for BP+OSD, but a small structured subset carries most of the suboptimality.} } \] If true, TN should be applied selectively to that subset.

6. The key comparison: logical-coset decision disagreement

For every sampled syndrome \(s\), run both decoders:

\[ \lambda_{\rm BP}(s) \]

from BP+OSD, and

\[ \lambda_{\rm TN}(s) = \arg\max_\lambda Z_\lambda(s) \]

from exact or controlled-accuracy TN contraction.

6.1 Disagreement indicator

\[ D(s) = \mathbf 1[ \lambda_{\rm BP}(s) \neq \lambda_{\rm TN}(s) ]. \]

The noise-weighted disagreement frequency is

\[ \boxed{ f_{\rm disagree}(p) = \sum_sP(s)\, D(s). } \]

If this is extremely small over the relevant \(p\)-range, TN offers little additional logical information under that noise model.

7. Not every disagreement matters: define coset regret

Suppose BP+OSD and TN disagree, but the two leading logical sectors have probabilities

\[ 0.501 \quad\text{and}\quad 0.499. \]

This is technically a disagreement but not a meaningful one.

Instead define the coset regret

\[ \boxed{ R(s) = P(\lambda_{\rm ML}|s) - P(\lambda_{\rm BP}|s). } \]

Large \(R(s)\) means BP+OSD selected a logical sector carrying substantially less posterior mass than the optimal one.

For example,

\[ P(\lambda_{\rm ML}|s)=0.80, \qquad P(\lambda_{\rm BP}|s)=0.08 \]

gives

\[ R(s)=0.72. \]

Those are the syndromes for which TN has a clear functional advantage.

8. Conditional decoding failure: TN gives a useful oracle

If the exact logical posterior is known, then for a fixed syndrome \(s\) the minimum achievable logical failure probability is

\[ \boxed{ p_{\rm fail}^{\rm ML}(s) = 1-\max_\lambda P(\lambda|s). } \]

If BP+OSD selects logical sector \(\lambda_{\rm BP}\), its conditional success probability is

\[ P(\lambda_{\rm BP}|s), \]

so its conditional failure probability is

\[ \boxed{ p_{\rm fail}^{\rm BP}(s) = 1-P(\lambda_{\rm BP}|s). } \]

Therefore the logical posterior allows us to quantify exactly how much performance is being lost on each syndrome.

9. Sweep the physical error rate \(p\)

The TN advantage should not be assumed to be monotonic in \(p\).

9.1 Very low \(p\)

The lowest-weight physical error typically dominates:

\[ p^w \gg M p^{w+1} \]

unless the multiplicity \(M\) is extremely large. BP+OSD is therefore expected to be difficult to beat in this limit.

9.2 Intermediate \(p\)

Higher-weight but highly degenerate contributions become more important:

\[ Z_\lambda = A_{\lambda,w}p^w + A_{\lambda,w+1}p^{w+1} +\cdots. \]

This is the most natural region in which entropy and logical-coset summation can change the decoding decision.

9.3 High \(p\)

The posterior over logical sectors may become broadly distributed, so even exact MLD may have a large irreducible logical failure probability.

The relevant observable is not simply “TN beats BP+OSD at some \(p\),” but the joint behavior of \[ f_{\rm disagree}(p), \qquad R(s), \qquad \Delta p_L(p), \] where \[ \Delta p_L(p) = p_L^{\rm BP+OSD}(p)-p_L^{\rm TN}(p). \]

10. Compare OSD candidate mass with the full coset mass

A natural objection is that OSD can generate more candidates. Therefore a fair experiment should quantify how much logical-coset probability is captured as the OSD list grows.

For an OSD candidate budget \(B\), define the truncated logical mass

\[ \widehat Z_\lambda^{(B)} = \sum_{\substack{ e\in\mathcal L_{\rm OSD}^{(B)}\\ e\in\lambda }} P(e). \]

Then define the coverage ratio

\[ \boxed{ C_\lambda^{(B)} = \frac{\widehat Z_\lambda^{(B)}} {Z_\lambda}. } \]

This directly answers:

  • Does OSD capture most of the important probability mass?
  • Is the coverage uniform across logical sectors?
  • Does the correct logical sector contain substantial probability outside the OSD list?

A particularly interesting failure mode would be:

\[ C_{\lambda_{\rm BP}}^{(B)} \approx 1, \]

but

\[ C_{\lambda_{\rm ML}}^{(B)} \ll 1. \]

That would show that the OSD search is systematically under-sampling the correct logical sector.

11. Compare approximation budgets fairly

TN is not automatically exact. Exact contraction may be too costly for BB72, in which case one introduces truncation or compressed contraction with a control parameter such as bond dimension \(\chi\).

The scientifically clean comparison is therefore not merely

\[ \text{OSD versus TN}, \]

but

\[ \boxed{ \text{OSD candidate budget }B \quad\text{versus}\quad \text{TN contraction budget }\chi. } \]

Useful axes include:

  • wall-clock time;
  • peak memory;
  • logical error rate;
  • logical posterior error;
  • total-variation distance to an exact reference where available.

For small codes or small hard subgraphs, exact enumeration can provide the ground truth.

12. TN as a maximum-likelihood oracle

Even if global TN is not competitive as a real-time decoder, it can still be valuable as a research oracle.

Suppose BP+OSD gives

\[ p_L^{\rm BP+OSD}=10^{-4}. \]

Without an MLD reference we do not know whether the code itself limits performance or whether the decoder is far from optimal.

If TN establishes

\[ p_L^{\rm ML}\approx10^{-4}, \]

then BP+OSD is already close to optimal.

If instead

\[ p_L^{\rm ML}\ll10^{-4}, \]

then there is a decoder gap worth understanding.

Therefore TN can help separate

\[ \boxed{ \text{code limitation} \quad\text{from}\quad \text{decoder limitation}. } \]

13. Failure detection from the logical posterior

The TN posterior contains more information than a single correction.

Define

\[ P_{\max}(s) = \max_\lambda P(\lambda|s), \]

the logical entropy

\[ H_L(s) = -\sum_\lambda P(\lambda|s)\log P(\lambda|s), \]

and the top-two posterior gap

\[ \Delta_{\rm post}(s) = P_{(1)}(s)-P_{(2)}(s). \]

These quantities can be used to determine whether a syndrome is easy or intrinsically ambiguous.

One can then compare their ability to predict BP+OSD failure against simpler diagnostics:

  • BP convergence flag;
  • minimum LLR magnitude;
  • number of low-reliability variables;
  • OSD best-versus-second-best candidate score gap.

This naturally leads to a selective decoder.

14. The most realistic architecture: BP+OSD plus selective TN

\[ \text{syndrome }s \] \[ \downarrow \] \[ \text{fast BP / BP+OSD} \] \[ \begin{cases} \text{high confidence / easy syndrome} &\rightarrow \text{output immediately},\\[2mm] \text{low confidence / hard syndrome} &\rightarrow \text{invoke TN refinement}. \end{cases} \] \[ \downarrow \] \[ \text{logical-coset refinement} \] \[ \downarrow \] \[ \text{final correction} \]

If hard syndromes are rare below threshold, the average cost can remain close to BP+OSD while the expensive TN calculation is reserved for the small subset where it can matter.

This architecture is more plausible than applying a global TN contraction to every syndrome.

15. Local TN rather than global TN

A still more practical extension is to use BP to identify a difficult region of the Tanner graph and perform TN contraction only on that subgraph.

A possible workflow is:

  1. Run BP and obtain qubit reliabilities / LLRs.
  2. Locate low-confidence variables, residual syndrome structure, or short-loop clusters.
  3. Extract a local Tanner subgraph around the difficult region.
  4. Convert external BP messages into boundary weights.
  5. Contract the local TN exactly or with controlled bond dimension.
  6. Use the local coset information to rerank or modify the BP+OSD decision.

Schematically,

\[ C_{\rm hybrid} \sim C_{\rm BP}(n) + f_{\rm trigger}\, C_{\rm TN}(m,\chi), \]

where \(m\ll n\) is the size of the hard region and \(f_{\rm trigger}\) is the fraction of syndromes that activate TN.

16. Correlated noise: a natural TN advantage

The iid code-capacity model may not be the regime in which TN shows its strongest advantage.

For independent noise,

\[ P(e) = \prod_iP_i(e_i). \]

Correlated noise can instead be written as a factor graph:

\[ P(e) \propto \prod_i\phi_i(e_i) \prod_{(i,j)}\psi_{ij}(e_i,e_j) \prod_{(i,j,k)}\psi_{ijk}(e_i,e_j,e_k) \cdots. \]

Such factors can be incorporated directly into the tensor network.

Relevant physical mechanisms include:

  • two-qubit gate error propagation;
  • spatially correlated Pauli errors;
  • shared-control or crosstalk events;
  • measurement-data correlations;
  • temporally correlated faults across syndrome rounds.
A strong TN project should not claim that BP cannot handle correlations. Rather, the benchmark should compare standard BP+OSD, correlation-aware BP variants, and TN under the same correlated noise model.

17. A particularly clean correlated-noise experiment

Construct two noise models with the same one-qubit marginals,

\[ P_A(e_i=1) = P_B(e_i=1) = p, \]

but different joint distributions,

\[ P_A(e_i,e_j) \neq P_B(e_i,e_j). \]

For example:

  • Model A: independent noise;
  • Model B: pair-correlated noise with tunable correlation strength \(J\).

Then examine whether the optimal logical sector changes with \(J\):

\[ Z_{\lambda_1}(s;J_c) = Z_{\lambda_2}(s;J_c). \]

A TN that explicitly incorporates the joint factors can follow this logical-sector crossing. A decoder supplied only with the same single-qubit priors cannot distinguish the two models unless its graph/model is correspondingly upgraded.

This experiment isolates the value of modeling the full joint distribution rather than merely improving candidate search.

18. TN can output a full logical posterior, not only a correction

BB72 encodes

\[ k=12 \]

logical qubits, so a pure \(X\)-error sector contains

\[ 2^{12}=4096 \]

logical classes.

Rather than returning only one correction, a logical-coset method can in principle provide

\[ P(\lambda|s) \qquad \text{for all }\lambda. \]

This enables additional tasks:

  • failure confidence estimation;
  • soft information for multi-stage decoding;
  • posterior calibration;
  • training labels for machine-learning decoders;
  • decoder-independent study of hard syndromes;
  • comparison of code constructions under the same noise model.

19. Research plan: Phase I — establish the oracle

Goal

Demonstrate that the TN implementation correctly computes logical-coset probabilities.

Tasks

  1. Use small CSS / small BB instances where exact enumeration is possible.
  2. Verify \[ Z_\lambda^{\rm TN}(s) = Z_\lambda^{\rm exact}(s). \]
  3. Verify posterior normalization.
  4. Verify that the selected correction leaves only a stabilizer when the true logical sector is chosen.
  5. Measure exact contraction width and memory growth.

Go criterion

TN reproduces exact logical-coset probabilities to numerical precision.

20. Phase II — measure the BP+OSD versus coset-MLD gap on BB72

Goal

Determine whether BB72 under iid code-capacity noise actually contains a meaningful decoder gap.

For each sampled error

  1. Generate \(e_{\rm true}\).
  2. Compute syndrome \(s\).
  3. Run BP+OSD and determine \(\lambda_{\rm BP}\).
  4. Compute or approximate \(Z_\lambda(s)\).
  5. Determine \(\lambda_{\rm ML}\).
  6. Record disagreement and coset regret.

Primary observables

\[ f_{\rm disagree}(p), \] \[ R(s), \] \[ p_L^{\rm BP+OSD}(p), \] \[ p_L^{\rm TN}(p), \] \[ \Delta p_L(p). \]

Go / No-Go

Go: a non-negligible subset of syndromes shows systematic, high-regret disagreement.

No-Go for global iid TN: BP+OSD and coset-MLD agree almost everywhere in the useful \(p\)-range.

21. Phase III — characterize the hard syndromes

Goal

Understand what structural features distinguish the high-regret syndromes.

Candidate features

  • BP convergence / iteration count;
  • LLR distribution;
  • number and geometry of low-confidence qubits;
  • short loops in the active Tanner subgraph;
  • residual syndrome clusters;
  • OSD list diversity;
  • logical posterior entropy;
  • top-two logical posterior gap.

Desired outcome

A simple rule or learned classifier that identifies when TN is worth invoking.

22. Phase IV — selective or local TN

Goal

Convert the oracle into a practical hybrid decoder.

Two variants

Selective global TN: invoke the full logical-coset calculation only for rare difficult syndromes.

Local TN: contract only the difficult subgraph identified by BP and absorb the rest of the code into boundary messages.

Key metric

\[ \boxed{ \text{logical-error reduction per unit of additional runtime / memory}. } \]

The target is not to beat BP+OSD in raw latency on every shot, but to recover a significant fraction of its logical failures at modest average overhead.

23. Phase V — correlated and circuit-derived noise

Goal

Move to a regime where the ability to represent structured joint probabilities is itself part of the decoding advantage.

Progression

  1. pair-correlated synthetic noise;
  2. spatially inhomogeneous correlations;
  3. combined \(X/Z/Y\) Pauli noise;
  4. phenomenological time correlations;
  5. circuit-derived detector error models.

Baselines

  • BP+OSD with independent priors;
  • correlation-aware BP / graph-augmented BP;
  • TN with explicit correlation factors;
  • exact reference on small instances where possible.

25. What would constitute a real TN contribution?

A convincing result does not need TN to dominate BP+OSD everywhere. Any of the following would already be meaningful:

  1. Oracle contribution: quantify the decoder gap between BP+OSD and logical-coset MLD for BB72.
  2. Mechanism contribution: identify the structural origin of high-regret BP+OSD failures.
  3. Hybrid-decoder contribution: show that selective/local TN repairs a significant subset of failures with modest average overhead.
  4. Noise-model contribution: show that TN captures correlated-noise logical posteriors more accurately than independent-prior decoding.
  5. Soft-information contribution: provide calibrated logical posteriors and uncertainty measures that standard correction-only decoding does not naturally supply.
  6. Code-design contribution: use logical-coset partition functions to compare BB-like codes independently of a particular heuristic decoder.

26. What should not be the main claim?

The least promising main claim is: \[ \boxed{ \text{“A global TN decoder is faster than BP+OSD for BB72 under iid noise.”} } \] There is no strong reason to expect that to be true, and even if a small implementation wins on one size, it is unlikely to capture the most interesting conceptual advantage of TN.

The more defensible claim is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{TN computes or approximates logical-coset probability information that candidate-based decoding does not explicitly sum,} } \]

and this information can be used where it matters: hard syndromes, decoder-gap diagnosis, selective refinement, and correlated noise.

27. Final research strategy

The project should proceed in the following order:

\[ \boxed{ \text{build a correct coset-probability TN} } \] \[ \Downarrow \] \[ \boxed{ \text{use it as an MLD oracle} } \] \[ \Downarrow \] \[ \boxed{ \text{measure the BP+OSD gap on BB72} } \] \[ \Downarrow \] \[ \boxed{ \text{identify high-regret hard syndromes} } \] \[ \Downarrow \] \[ \boxed{ \text{develop selective/local TN refinement} } \] \[ \Downarrow \] \[ \boxed{ \text{extend to correlated and circuit-derived noise} } \]

The decision point is simple:

If iid BB72 shows almost no coset-MLD gap, do not force TN into that regime. Move to the regimes where full probability structure matters.

If a structured hard-syndrome gap exists, then TN has a clear role: not as a universal replacement for BP+OSD, but as a targeted logical-coset inference tool.

That is the most credible path for demonstrating a genuine tensor-network contribution to BB72 decoding.
