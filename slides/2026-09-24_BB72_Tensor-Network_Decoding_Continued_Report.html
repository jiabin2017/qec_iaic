BB72 Tensor-Network Decoding Continued

H100 LER benchmark, block-size optimization, and multi-syndrome validation. Report dated 24 September 2026.
Bottom line. The complete 30,000-shot H100 logical-error-rate sweep reproduces the A100 logical results while reducing average full-posterior shot time from about 182.4 s to 143.8 s. Hardware acceleration alone does not remove the cost of 4095 scalar tensor-network queries per shot. On the exact branch-and-bound route, block-size optimization changes the dominant computation: the original (b=3) layout required 1.878 s and 3.414 s on the easy and hard reference syndromes, whereas the selected (b=9) layout requires about 0.208 s and 0.393 s. A subsequent 360,000-decode validation confirms that (b=9) identity remains the fastest tested layout across a broad syndrome distribution.

0. Scope and experimental pipelines

This report continues the 18 September v0.3 engineering report. It covers two related but distinct BB72 tensor-network pipelines. The first completes the earlier scalar parity-query plus Walsh–Hadamard reconstruction program on H100 GPUs and compares it with the full A100 result. The second continues exact logical-sector branch-and-bound from the fixed (b=3) baseline through block-size optimization and broad multi-syndrome validation.

1. H100 LER branch and complete A100 comparison

1.1 Experiment design and integrity

Both sweeps decode the same BB72 ([[72,12,6]]) problem at six physical error rates, with 5,000 shots at each point. The H100 run uses the same deterministic seeds and therefore the same 30,000 physical errors and true logical sectors as the A100 run. Each shot loads all 4095 cached contraction paths, evaluates every nonzero logical mask, and reconstructs the complete sector posterior using an inverse Walsh–Hadamard transform.

1.4 Numerical consistency

The two sweeps agree on all 30,000 true sectors and on the logical success/failure outcome for 29,984 shots. Only 22 shots select different MAP sectors; every one is an extreme numerical tie, with the largest recorded top-1/top-2 gap only (1.16 imes10^{-14}). The small differences in aggregate failure counts therefore reflect floating-point contraction order and deterministic tie-breaking rather than a physical change in the decoder.

At (p=0.005), both platforms encounter the same 20 failures of the CUDA-QX public probability path. All 20 are recovered by the raw-TN fallback. On H100 the largest relative clipped negative mass is (1.12 imes10^{-12}), and all 30,000 final records remain valid.

LER-branch conclusion. H100 improves the scalar-WHT pipeline, but the inverse WHT is not the bottleneck. The dominant cost is issuing thousands of separate tensor-network queries. This result strengthens the motivation for a multi-logical exact search that avoids full-posterior reconstruction.

2. v0.3 exact-BnB baseline

The v0.3 decoder groups the 12 logical variables into four consecutive blocks of three: 

block size = 3
layout     = [0..2] | [3..5] | [6..8] | [9..11]

Each oracle contraction returns (2^3=8) child subtree masses. The search visits high-mass children first, establishes an incumbent complete-sector mass, and prunes every subtree whose total probability mass cannot beat that incumbent.

The next performance question is no longer whether exact pruning works. It is how many logical variables should be opened in one oracle call. A small block has a small output tensor but creates more search levels and repeated contractions. A large block reduces search depth but exposes a larger output tensor and may create a more expensive contraction topology.

4.2 Why the extended curve is non-monotonic

Block size alone does not determine contraction cost. Tensor connectivity, largest intermediates, slicing, path quality, and overlap among physical supports all change when a different logical set is exposed. Consequently (b=7), (b=8), and (b=11) are much slower than neighboring configurations even though they use fewer BnB levels than smaller blocks.

The (b=12) control illustrates the opposite extreme: one call creates the complete 4096-sector tensor and therefore gives up adaptive pruning. It is faster than repeatedly contracting one logical bit at a time, but slower than the best partial-output layouts. The (b=9) layout exposes 512 prefixes in the root contraction and leaves only three logical bits for each surviving tail query, achieving the best measured balance.

5. v0.5: 360,000-decode multi-syndrome validation

5.1 Why broader validation was required

The easy and hard syndromes are effective correctness and regression tests, but they cannot characterize a decoder's runtime distribution. v0.5 therefore freezes paired physical errors and syndromes, then evaluates the same samples under several block layouts. The experiment measures whether the (b=9) winner survives a broad natural-noise distribution and fixed-weight stress cases rather than only the original two examples.

One (b=9) root call produces 512 prefix masses. A typical shot then explores only the highest-mass prefix and executes one three-bit tail call returning eight complete sectors. An exhaustive 9+3 traversal would require (1+512=513) block-oracle calls; the observed natural mean of 2.0359 calls eliminates approximately 99.60% of those calls. This is a call-count comparison within the same block oracle, not a direct FLOP or wall-time ratio.

As natural (p) increases from 0.005 to 0.05, mean (b=9) latency rises only from 0.2075 s to 0.2176 s, about 4.9%. P99 rises from 0.2076 s to 0.3913 s, showing that higher physical error rates mainly create a small tail of difficult syndromes rather than slowing every decode.

Against the (b=12) full-output control, (b=9) selects a different sector in 103 of 30,000 natural cases and 271 of 30,000 stress cases. The natural mismatches are concentrated in near-ties: among the sector-different natural shots, the maximum difference between the two reported best probabilities is (3.744 imes10^{-13}). The stress comparison contains 125 cases whose best-probability difference exceeds (10^{-10}), with a maximum of approximately (3.30 imes10^{-8}).

Numerical interpretation. The (b=12) control is itself a float64 cuTensorNet contraction, not an independent high-precision certificate. Sector mismatch counts therefore cannot be interpreted directly as BnB algorithmic errors, and small LER differences between layouts do not establish different physical decoding quality. The present result validates the performance advantage while preserving an explicit numerical qualification for near-degenerate cases.

6. Conclusions

\[ \boxed{ \text{The BB72 exact-TN baseline moves from fixed }b=3\text{ to a validated }b=9\text{ identity layout.} } \]

The complete H100 sweep confirms that faster hardware reduces the cost of full-posterior reconstruction but does not change its fundamental 4095-query burden. P5 instead changes the computation itself. The initial block scan identifies the benefit of reducing search depth, the extended scan locates the 9+3 optimum, and the 360,000-decode campaign confirms that this choice remains fastest across natural and stress syndrome distributions.

  • H100 full-posterior result: the LER curve agrees with A100, while average shot time falls from about 182.4 s to 143.8 s.
  • Initial P5 result: scanning (b=1,2,3,4,6) selects (b=6), reducing the recorded easy/hard timing to 1.069/1.973 s.
  • Extended P5 result: the (b=9) identity layout reaches 0.208/0.393 s on the reference syndromes, approximately 9.0×/8.7× faster than v0.3.
  • Broad validation: (b=9) identity is fastest in every tested data condition and averages 0.2109 s on natural syndromes, 12.12× faster than the (b=12) full-output control.
  • Current BB72 baseline: retain the validated P0–P5 implementation with logical layout [0..8] | [9..11], identity order, GPU-resident operands, and compiled contraction profiles.
Engineering conclusion. The main gain after v0.3 does not come from another allocator or cache optimization. It comes from selecting the right logical output geometry: expose enough logical variables to collapse search depth, but retain a small tail so branch-and-bound can avoid the cost of a complete 4096-sector contraction.
